# UPSC CMS Interview (Personality Test) and DAF Guidelines

This document details the complete roadmap for the UPSC Combined Medical Services (CMS) interview stage (Personality Test), which carries 100 marks out of the final 600. It covers service preferences, Detailed Application Form (DAF) guidance, and actual candidate question transcripts.

---

## 1. THE DETAILED APPLICATION FORM (DAF)

After qualifying in the written exam, candidates must submit **DAF-1** and **DAF-2** online via the UPSC portal.

### **Key Sections of DAF:**
1. **Personal Profile**: Date of Birth, Place of Birth, Home State, Father's/Mother's profession and income.
2. **Educational Background**: MBBS marks list, university, year of passing, college reputation, school achievements.
3. **Service Preferences (DAF-2)**:
   * **Category I**:
     * Assistant Divisional Medical Officer (ADMO) in Indian Railways.
     * Medical Officer in Central Health Service (CHS) - CGHS, Postings in Delhi, hospitals.
   * **Category II**:
     * Medical Officer in New Delhi Municipal Council (NDMC).
     * Assistant Medical Officer in East/North/South Delhi Municipal Corporations (MCD).
     * General Duty Medical Officer (GDMO) in Delhi Administration.
4. **Extra-Curricular Activities & Hobbies**: Hobbies listed here (e.g., jogging, playing flute, reading clinical research, teaching) are heavily targeted by the Chairperson.

---

## 2. INTERVIEW DAY EXPERIENCE (STEP-BY-STEP)

1. **Reporting & Security**: Candidates report at UPSC Dholpur House, New Delhi. Mobile phones, electronic items, and bags are deposited.
2. **Waiting Hall & Verification**: Candidates are seated in a central waiting hall. Original documents are checked:
   * MBBS Degree / Provisional Certificate.
   * Intern Completion Certificate.
   * Date of Birth Proof (10th/12th certificate).
   * Caste/EWS certificate (if applicable).
   * Document verification for OBC (Non-Creamy Layer) is extremely strict.
3. **Panel Allocation**: Candidates are assigned a panel number (often named after the Board Chairperson, who is a UPSC Member).
4. **The Personality Test (Interview)**:
   * Duration: **15 - 30 minutes**.
   * Panel Composition: 1 Chairperson (UPSC IAS/IFS member) + 3 Medical Specialists (Professors/Senior Doctors in Medicine, Surgery, OBG, PSM).
5. **Medical Examination**: Conducted the day after the interview at designated government hospitals in Delhi (e.g., Safdarjung Hospital, Dr. RML Hospital, Lok Nayak Hospital). This checks color blindness, systemic health, and physical fitness for various cadres.

## 3. ACTUAL CANDIDATE INTERVIEW QUESTION TRANSCRIPTS

### **Board Chairperson Questions (General & HR):**
1. **Q: Introduce yourself in 2 minutes highlighting your medical background.**
   * *Tip*: Connect your MBBS college location, family background, and why you opted for clinical services.
2. **Q: Why choose UPSC CMS over a private corporate hospital or a PG seat?**
   * *Best Answer*: Highlight the opportunity to work at the grassroots, participate in national healthcare policy implementation (like Ayushman Bharat), and gain diverse clinical exposure that private hospitals do not provide.
3. **Q: If selected for ADMO in Indian Railways, how will you manage a medical emergency on a moving passenger train with limited equipment?**
   * *Best Answer*: Focus on basic triage, stabilization of vitals, securing airway/breathing, calling for local support at the next station, and using the emergency drug kit available on trains.
4. **Q: What is your opinion on the brain drain of Indian doctors migrating abroad?**
   * *Best Answer*: Present a balanced view, acknowledging the search for better training, but emphasizing that with recent infrastructure development and NMC revisions, India offers outstanding opportunities.

### **Medical Specialist 1 Questions (Medicine & Pediatrics):**
1. **Q: How will you clinically differentiate between DKA (Diabetic Ketoacidosis) and HHS (Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic State) at a primary health centre?**
   * *Answer*: Check breathing (Kussmaul breathing is present in DKA, absent in HHS). DKA has rapid onset with positive urine ketones and mild-to-moderate hyperglycemia (<600 mg/dL). HHS has gradual onset, extreme dehydration, altered mental status, glucose >600 mg/dL, and trace/no ketones.
2. **Q: What is the drug of choice for a child presenting with status epilepticus? State the dose and route.**
   * *Answer*: Intravenous Lorazepam (0.1 mg/kg) administered over 1-2 minutes. If IV access is unavailable, per-rectal Diazepam (0.5 mg/kg) or intranasal Midazolam (0.2 mg/kg) can be given.
3. **Q: If a child comes to you at 9 months of age, which vaccines will you administer under the Universal Immunization Programme (UIP)?**
   * *Answer*: MR (Measles-Rubella) 1st dose, Japanese Encephalitis (JE) 1st dose (in endemic districts), Vitamin A 1st dose (1 Lakh IU), and PCV (Pneumococcal Conjugate) Booster dose.

### **Medical Specialist 2 Questions (Surgery & OBG):**
1. **Q: What is the Alvarado score? Tell me the components.**
   * *Answer*: Alvarado score is used to diagnose acute appendicitis. Score >= 7 indicates surgery. Components (MANTRELS): Migration of pain to RIF (1), Anorexia (1), Nausea/Vomiting (1), Tenderness in RIF (2), Rebound tenderness (1), Elevated temperature (1), Leukocytosis (2), Shift to left of neutrophils (1).
2. **Q: Explain the management of Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH) using the 4 Ts.**
   * *Answer*: Identify cause: Tone (Atony - 70%), Trauma (lacerations), Tissue (retained placenta), Thrombin (coagulopathy). If Atonic: Perform uterine massage, administer Oxytocin (10-20 units in IV infusion), Methergine (0.2mg IM, contra-indicated in HTN), Carboprost (250mcg IM), or Misoprostol (800mcg rectal). If refractory, use Bakri Balloon, B-Lynch suture, or Internal Iliac Ligation.
3. **Q: What are the clinical parameters to monitor when a patient is on Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO4) therapy for Eclampsia?**
   * *Answer*: Monitor three key parameters before each maintenance dose: 1) Deep tendon reflexes (Patellar reflex must be present), 2) Respiratory rate (must be >16 breaths/min), 3) Urine output (must be >25-30 mL/hr or >100 mL in 4 hours).

### **Medical Specialist 3 Questions (PSM & Allied):**
1. **Q: What is the difference between Sensitivity and Specificity? If a test has high sensitivity, what does it rule out?**
   * *Answer*: Sensitivity is the probability of a test being positive when the disease is present. Specificity is the probability of a test being negative when the disease is absent. High sensitivity means a negative result rules OUT the disease (SnNOut). High specificity means a positive result rules IN the disease (SpPIn).
2. **Q: What is the current daily regimen for Tuberculosis under the NTEP?**
   * *Answer*: Daily regimen of Fixed Dose Combinations (FDCs). Intensive Phase (2 months): Isoniazid (H), Rifampicin (R), Pyrazinamide (Z), Ethambutol (E). Continuation Phase (4 months): Isoniazid (H), Rifampicin (R), Ethambutol (E).
3. **Q: How do you segregate Biomedical Waste in a clinical ward?**
   * *Answer*: Yellow: Human anatomical waste, blood bags, soiled gauze (Incineration). Red: Recyclable plastics like syringes without needles, IV sets, catheters (Autoclave). White: Sharp metals, needles, scalpels (Puncture-proof container, autoclaving & shredding). Blue: Glass vials, ampoules, metallic body implants (Sodium hypochlorite treatment or autoclaving).
